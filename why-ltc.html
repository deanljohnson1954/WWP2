The Planning Imperative

Why Long-Term Care Planning Matters

An objective examination of a financial risk that most retirement plans overlook, underestimate, or misunderstand.

Common Misconception

Medicare Will Not Cover Extended Care

Medicare is designed to cover acute and rehabilitative care — not the sustained custodial care that most long-term care events require.

Medicare may cover a limited stay in a skilled nursing facility following a qualifying hospitalization. However, it does not cover:

  • Ongoing home health aide services for custodial care
  • Assisted living facilities
  • Memory care facilities
  • Adult day programs providing supervision
  • Personal care assistance with activities of daily living

Medi-Cal (California's Medicaid program) does cover long-term care — but only after most personal assets have been spent down to eligibility levels. This is not a strategy appropriate for professionals with meaningful retirement assets.

Medicare Coverage Summary

Acute hospital care Covered
Short-term skilled nursing (post-hospitalization) Limited
Custodial home care Not Covered
Assisted living facilities Not Covered
Memory care facilities Not Covered
Extended personal care assistance Not Covered
California Cost Reality

What Long-Term Care Actually Costs Here

National cost averages significantly understate the financial exposure facing Bay Area families. These are the numbers that belong in your retirement plan.

Home Care

Professional Home Health Aide

Full-time home care in the Bay Area commonly ranges from $8,000 to $15,000 per month or more, depending on hours, acuity, and care team composition.

Facility

Assisted Living

Quality assisted living facilities in the Bay Area range from $7,000 to $12,000+ per month. Memory care units — which require specialized staffing — command a significant premium.

Memory Care

Specialized Dementia Care

Dedicated memory care facilities in the San Francisco Bay Area can exceed $12,000 to $15,000 per month, with quality facilities often at the upper end of that range.

"At $12,500 per month, a five-year care event represents $750,000 in direct care costs — not including family time, coordinating physicians, or ancillary services. A ten-year event doubles that. This is a quantifiable retirement risk, not an abstract concern."

Risk Factors

What Makes This Risk Distinctive

Duration

Claims Can Span Years or Decades

Unlike most insurable risks that resolve in a defined period, long-term care claims — especially dementia-related — can remain open for a decade or more, making cost projection genuinely difficult without structured coverage.

Home Care Shift

Most Care Now Occurs at Home

The majority of long-term care today is delivered by professional aides in the home, not in nursing facilities. Plans designed primarily around facility benefits may significantly underperform in practice.

Gender Asymmetry

Women Require Care Longer

Women live longer than men on average and are statistically more likely to spend extended periods in care, often as a surviving spouse. Couples planning that treats both partners identically misses a material actuarial reality.

Insurability

Health Gates Tighten With Age

Long-term care insurance requires medical underwriting. The older and less healthy an applicant, the fewer carriers will offer coverage and the higher the premiums. The optimal planning window is well before most families act.

Family Impact

Unstructured Care Burdens Families

Without a structured plan, care coordination typically falls to adult children — often at significant personal and professional cost. This is a family planning issue as much as a financial one.

Inflation

Care Costs Inflate Rapidly

Long-term care costs have historically increased faster than general inflation. Plans that do not address benefit inflation risk a growing gap between coverage and actual care costs over a multi-decade retirement.
