Sophisticated Solutions for Complex Needs

No single product or carrier is appropriate for every client. Independent, comparative analysis across structures produces meaningfully better outcomes.

Why Independence Matters

Carrier-Neutral. Analysis-First.

Most professionals who sell long-term care insurance represent one or a small number of carriers. Their recommendations are necessarily constrained by what they can offer.

An independent analysis reviews the full universe of available structures — hybrid life and LTC, asset-based designs, traditional LTC, and combination approaches — and matches the appropriate strategy to each client's specific financial profile, health status, and planning objectives.

This distinction is not subtle. Carrier-specific advisors and independent analysts often arrive at materially different recommendations for the same client.

Planning Structures

Five Approaches to Long-Term Care Risk

Strategy 01

Hybrid Life & LTC Strategies

Guaranteed premiums Death benefit if care not used No benefit forfeiture

Hybrid life and LTC policies combine a life insurance death benefit with long-term care protection in a single contract. Premiums are typically guaranteed never to increase. If LTC benefits are never used, a death benefit passes to beneficiaries — a feature that makes the economics significantly more attractive than traditional LTC insurance for many clients.

Certain hybrid structures offer cash indemnity benefits — meaning benefits are paid directly without the requirement to submit receipts or pre-approve care providers. This design provides maximum flexibility for clients who want control over how care is arranged and delivered.

Select carriers now offer hybrid structures that can be funded using IRA or 401(k) assets, allowing qualified retirement funds to be repositioned to cover care costs on a tax-advantaged basis. Lifetime benefit options are also available for clients seeking protection against extended care events without a defined benefit pool cap.

Strategy 02

Asset-Based LTC Planning

Repositions existing assets Leveraged LTC benefit Single or periodic premium

Asset-based LTC planning involves repositioning existing assets — often low-yield savings, CDs, or underperforming portfolio holdings — into a structure that provides long-term care benefits on a leveraged basis. A single premium payment generates a multiple of the deposit as an available LTC benefit pool.

For clients who are uncomfortable with ongoing premium commitments or who prefer not to perceive their LTC coverage as an "expense," this structure can be particularly effective. The underlying assets remain accessible in many designs, and the net cost of coverage is often modest relative to the benefit leverage achieved.

This approach works particularly well for clients with underutilized non-qualified savings, inherited assets, or recent liquidity events who want to deploy capital purposefully toward a defined risk.

Strategy 03

Lifetime Benefit Designs

No benefit pool cap Protects against longest events Dementia-appropriate

Standard LTC and hybrid policies define a total benefit pool — a maximum dollar amount of benefits available over the life of the policy. For typical care events, this is usually adequate. For the longest events — particularly advanced dementia — a fixed benefit pool may be exhausted years before the claim concludes.

Lifetime benefit designs eliminate the benefit pool cap. Benefits continue for as long as the insured remains claim-eligible, regardless of duration or total amount paid. Only a small number of carriers currently offer genuine lifetime benefit options, and the structures vary meaningfully in design and flexibility.

For clients with family history of dementia or Alzheimer's disease, or for those who simply want to eliminate duration risk entirely, a lifetime benefit design deserves serious analytical consideration.

Strategy 04

Executive Protection Strategies

High monthly benefit levels Business/personal coordination Tax-efficient structures

High-income professionals require LTC planning that reflects their actual cost of living and care expectations. Most standard LTC benefit levels — designed for the general market — are insufficient for executives and senior professionals accustomed to premium-quality care.

Executive protection strategies are designed to provide meaningful monthly benefit levels (commonly $12,500–$15,000 or more), coordinate with deferred compensation structures, and address income replacement considerations during a care event. Business owners may also benefit from tax-advantaged benefit funding arrangements.

This planning also addresses the frequently overlooked transition from professional income to retirement income during a care event, when income replacement and care cost coverage may operate simultaneously.

Strategy 05

Multi-Carrier Comparative Analysis

Independent of any carrier AM Best-rated carriers only Benefit and pricing comparison

The starting point for every engagement is a structured comparison of leading carriers across multiple dimensions: product structure, benefit flexibility, financial strength rating, premium stability history, claim experience, and pricing competitiveness for the client's specific age and health profile.

This analysis is available in a one-page illustrated format that allows direct comparison across carriers without promotional language or carrier-specific bias. The output is an objective ranking of which structures and carriers are most appropriate for the specific client situation.

Because no single carrier is optimal for every client, the value of independent analysis compounds with the complexity of the client's financial profile.
